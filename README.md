# yamDetector

Sound-awareness system. A wearable harness (Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense with 4 PDM MEMS mics,
two on the chest and two on the back) classifies urgent sound on the device and works out which
side of the wearer it came from. An ESP-01 wristband with a vibration motor and an OLED receives
those alerts over ESP-NOW: it buzzes and shows what the sound is and where it came from.

Thirteen classes, in two tiers:

| Tier | Classes | On the band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **alert** | `siren`, `car_horn`, `alarm`, `knock`, `glass`, `scream`, `baby_cry`, `dog_bark`, `gunshot`, `thunder` | buzzes, names the sound and the direction |
| **silent** | `speech`, `music`, `background` | nothing: they exist so the model can explain ordinary room sound instead of forcing it onto an urgent class |

Two models are in the repo, and `MODEL_CLASSES` at the top of `room_capture/yam_infer.h` picks
one. Both send the same class ids, so the wristband never needs reflashing for a model change.

| `MODEL_CLASSES` | Model | Detects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `5` (default) | `model_5class.h`, the original ESC-50 model | `siren`, `car_horn`, `alarm`, `knock`. It is the default because it is the one that detects reliably on the harness today |
| `13` | `model.h`, the FSD50K + RAVDESS retrain | all ten alert classes, but it is less sure of itself and mistakes some alarms for music |

**For the demo only `siren` and `knock` buzz the band**, whichever model runs. `ALERT_ON` at the
top of `yam_infer.h` lists the classes that may fire. The others still print their probability on
the harness's Serial Monitor but never send an alert. Add a `CLS_*` name there to bring one back.

| Working | Not built yet |
| --- | --- |
| 4-mic capture, 0 dropped blocks | |
| Recording training clips to the laptop | |
| On-device int8 classifier printing to Serial | |
| Direction: front / right / behind / left (section 4) | |
| ESP-NOW alerts to the wristband: buzz + class + direction on the OLED | |

The model is a DS-CNN trained with TensorFlow/Keras on ESC-50, FSD50K, RAVDESS and our own
recordings, exported to int8 TFLite Micro.

## 1. Wiring

Harness, XIAO ESP32S3 Sense:

| Signal | XIAO pin | Mics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PDM clock | D0 (GPIO1) | all four, shared |
| PDM data 0 | D1 (GPIO2) | mics 1 and 2 |
| PDM data 1 | D3 (GPIO4) | mics 3 and 4 |
| SEL | GND for mics 1 and 3, 3V3 for mics 2 and 4 | |
| VCC / GND | 3V3 / GND | all |

Avoid D2 (strapping pin) and D8-D10 (used by the Sense's SD slot). The classifier listens to mic 1.

Where each mic is worn. Direction finding depends on this, so if you move a mic, update the
`MIC_*` constants at the top of `room_capture/doa.h`:

| Mic | Position | Data line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | chest, wearer's right | D1 |
| 2 | chest, wearer's left | D1 |
| 3 | back, wearer's right | D3 |
| 4 | back, wearer's left | D3 |

The four mics form a 30 x 30 cm square around the torso: 30 cm across the chest, 30 cm across
the back, and 30 cm from chest to back on each side.

Wristband, ESP-01. The only 3.3 V board in the system: **2x AA, no regulator, never 5 V**.

| Part | Choice | Why this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radio board | ESP-01 (ESP8266EX, 1 MB flash), ESP-01S is the same | 8 pins, ESP-NOW peer of the harness, and we already have them |
| Display | 0.96" 128x64 I2C OLED, SSD1306, address 0x3C | two wires, no backlight circuit, 3.3 V |
| Haptics | 10 mm coin vibration motor, about 70 mA at 3 V | fits a wrist strap, enough to feel through a sleeve |
| Motor driver | BC337 (2N3904 works too), 1 kΩ base resistor, 1N4148 flyback | a GPIO can only source 12 mA and the motor is inductive |
| Power | 2x AA alkaline | matches the 3.3 V rail without a converter |
| Bulk | 100 µF electrolytic + 100 nF ceramic across VCC/GND | rides out the Wi-Fi TX burst and the motor start |
| Programmer | an ESP32 DevKit V1 | USB-serial pass-through, no extra adapter |
| Libraries | Adafruit SSD1306 + Adafruit GFX Library | drawn by `wristband.ino` |

Pinout, ESP-01 header (the silkscreen names are in the first column):

| Signal | ESP-01 pin | GPIO | Goes to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.3 V | VCC | | battery +, plus 100 µF / 100 nF to GND |
| Ground | GND | | battery -, OLED GND, BC337 emitter |
| Chip enable | CH_PD | | 3V3, otherwise the chip never starts |
| I2C data | GPIO0 | 0 | OLED SDA |
| I2C clock | GPIO2 | 2 | OLED SCL |
| Motor | RX | 3 | 1 kΩ to the BC337 base, motor between 3V3 and the collector |
| Serial debug | TX | 1 | DevKit RX, 115200 while the band is on the programmer |
| Reset | RST | | not used: the board has no buttons, pull a cell to power-cycle |

The motor branch in full: GPIO3 → 1 kΩ → BC337 base, BC337 emitter to GND, motor from 3V3 down to
the collector, and the 1N4148 across the motor with its cathode (banded end) on 3V3. Add 10 kΩ
from the base to GND: GPIO3 floats until the sketch claims it, and without the resistor the
motor can twitch at power-up.

Why it is wired this way:

- The ESP-01 has four usable GPIOs and the OLED needs two, so the motor gets GPIO3/RX and debug
  output keeps GPIO1/TX. `wristband.ino` takes GPIO3 back from the UART with `pinMode()` after
  `Serial.begin()`, so the band prints but has no serial input.
- The finished board has nothing to press: no reset button, no boot button, no header sticking
  out. Flash mode is GPIO0 held low at the moment power arrives, and a restart is a cell pulled
  for a second. `wristband.ino` is built for that, so `setup()` never halts: if the radio or the
  OLED fails to start it is retried from `loop()` every 2 s and 5 s.
- GPIO0 and GPIO2 are boot straps that must be high at boot. The OLED module's I2C pull-ups
  (usually 10 kΩ) hold them there, which is why no extra resistors appear above; if your module
  has none, add 10 kΩ from each line to 3V3. GPIO0 to GND still forces flash mode while flashing.
- 2x AA is 3.1-3.2 V fresh and browns out the ESP-01 around 2.9 V. The screen says `LOW BAT`
  below that. 3x AA (4.5 V) would kill the board, and 2x NiMH (2.4 V) needs a boost converter.
- The OLED address is 0x3C on almost every module; `OLED_ADDR` at the top of the sketch covers 0x3D.

Flash it through the ESP32 DevKit V1: see the header of `bringup/esp01_hello/esp01_hello.ino`.

## 3. Train and export

```bash
python scripts/prepare_esc50.py                 # ESC-50 -> data/processed (folds 1-3 train, 4 val, 5 test)
python scripts/prepare_datasets.py              # FSD50K + RAVDESS (splits by clip / by actor)
python scripts/convert_recordings.py            # optional: adds my_recordings_raw/<class>/*
python train.py                                 # features are cached, reruns start fast
python export.py --firmware-dir room_capture    # artifacts/ + room_capture/model.h
```

Useful options:

```bash
python scripts/prepare_esc50.py --limit 50      # smoke test (splits will be incomplete)
python scripts/prepare_datasets.py --limit-clips 50   # smoke test, one dataset or both
python scripts/prepare_datasets.py --source ravdess   # rebuild just one extra source
python scripts/convert_recordings.py --mix      # average all channels (default: channel 0)
python scripts/convert_recordings.py --channel 1
python train.py --epochs 10 --batch-size 8      # quick experiment
python train.py --no-augment --force-features   # no augmentation, rebuild the feature cache
```

Every stage can be re-run safely. Each prep script replaces only its own rows, and stage 3
reuses cached features.

**Class names are the contract.** `export.py` writes the model's output names into `model.h`
(`kYamLabels`), and `yam_infer.h` matches them to `CLASS_NAMES` in
`libraries/protocol/protocol.h` to get the class id it sends. So a model can have fewer
classes than `protocol.h`, or list them in another order, and nothing else changes. Every model
class does have to be in `CLASS_NAMES`, and `classes.alert` must match `CLASS_ALERT` there:
`export.py` warns about both, and the harness refuses to start on a name it doesn't know. To add
a class, append it after `background` in `protocol.h` (never renumber) and reflash both boards.
After exporting, set `MODEL_CLASSES` to anything but `5` to run the new `model.h`.

## 4. Run the harness

Open `room_capture/room_capture.ino`, pick a `MODE` at the top, and upload.

| `MODE` | What it does | Watch with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MODE_LEVELS` | level meter per mic and the measured data rate | Serial Monitor |
| `MODE_STREAM` | binary audio stream for recording training clips | `host/recorder.py` (Serial Monitor closed) |
| `MODE_INFER` | on-device classifier, and every Event over ESP-NOW to the wristband | Serial Monitor (any baud, native USB) |

With new mics, bring them up in that order. In `MODE_LEVELS`, snap your fingers next to each
mic: only its meter should jump. If the wrong meter moves, fix `SLOT_FOR_MIC`.

In `MODE_INFER` the alerts go out on ESP-NOW channel 1 as `msg_t` from `protocol.h`: an
`MSG_ALERT` per Event (class id, confidence, direction), a heartbeat every 5 s, and one `MSG_TEST` at
boot. The destination is `WRIST_ADDR` at the top of `room_capture.ino`, broadcast by default, so
the two boards need no pairing step.

Command-line upload:

```powershell
arduino-cli compile --upload -p COM11 --fqbn esp32:esp32:XIAO_ESP32S3:CDCOnBoot=default,PSRAM=opi --libraries libraries room_capture
```

`MODE_INFER` output:

```text
[yamDetector] radio: ESP-NOW channel 1 (radio on 1), alerts broadcast
[yamDetector] ready: model 44928 B, 5 classes, arena used 52112 of 524288 B (PSRAM)
[yamDetector] alerts on: siren 0.70 knock 0.70
[yamDetector] direction: on, 4 directions
siren 0.01  car_horn 0.02  alarm 0.01  knock 0.03  background 0.93  | gain 10.0 | 38 ms
siren 0.91  car_horn 0.04  alarm 0.03  knock 0.00  background 0.02  | gain  2.1 | 38 ms
[yamDetector] dir behind   az 187 | front/behind -0.71 (time -0.62, level  -9.6 dB) | right/left -0.08 (time -0.10, level  -0.7 dB) | 54 ms
[yamDetector] Event: SIREN | Conf: 91% | Dir: BEHIND | #1
[yamDetector] wristband ACK seq 43, 18 ms
```

(Illustrative output for the default `MODEL_CLASSES 5`. `model 44928 B` and the thresholds are
the real values; the `arena used` and millisecond figures depend on the build. With
`MODEL_CLASSES 13` the ready line says `model 142952 B, 13 classes`, each probability line lists
all thirteen classes, and a window takes about 117 ms.)

- One line per window: 1 s of audio, every 0.5 s. `gain` is the peak-normalization boost, and
  the last number is the processing time.
- An **Event** line prints when the top class is listed in `ALERT_ON` (siren and knock for now),
  its probability reaches that class's `CLASS_THRESHOLD`, and that class hasn't fired within
  `COOLDOWN_MS`. That same Event is the alert the wristband buzzes on. Every other class is
  printed but never fires. If an unlisted class such as `car_horn` comes out on top, nothing
  fires, even if siren is second.
- **Thresholds are per class and per model** (`CLASS_THRESHOLD` in `yam_infer.h`). The 5-class
  model is confident, so 0.70 fits all four of its classes. The 13-class model is trained with
  label smoothing, so even clear alarms and sirens peak around 0.7 (bar 0.40), while thunder and
  dog_bark also fire on rumble and noise (bar 0.80); those values come from ESC-50 fold 5, which
  neither model trained on. Raise a class's value if it buzzes falsely, and lower it if real
  sounds of that class print the right top class without an Event.
- The **ACK** line is the band echoing the message number and its timestamp, so the last number
  is the round trip. No ACK line means the band is off, out of range, or on another channel.
- These settings are at the top of `room_capture/yam_infer.h`. `PRINT_PROBS 0` leaves only
  the Event lines.
- Quick test: play ESC-50 fold-5 siren and door_wood_knock clips from a speaker; each should give
  an Event line. Then play car_horn and clock_alarm clips and talk: those can move the
  probabilities, but must not produce an Event line.

### Direction

Every Event says where the sound came from, relative to the wearer (chest = front):
`FRONT`, `RIGHT`, `BEHIND` or `LEFT`, or `UNKNOWN` when the evidence is too weak to call.
`room_capture/doa.h` uses the same 1 s window the classifier saw, from all four mics, and keeps
only the loudest 32 ms frames.

The torso sits between the chest and back mics and bends the sound around it, so the usual
"solve for an exact angle" maths doesn't hold. Instead it answers two questions, front or behind
and right or left, each from two cues:

- **Timing** (GCC-PHAT between mic pairs): which side heard it first. The nearer mic always
  wins, body or not. This is sharp on knocks and horns. On a pure tone the 30 cm spacing makes it
  ambiguous, and the cue then drops to about 0 by itself.
- **Level** (1-6 kHz): which side is louder. The body shadows the far side by several dB, and
  this keeps working on pure tones such as alarm beeps.

Each question gets a score from -1 to +1. The stronger question decides, and a score below
`DOA_MIN_SCORE` is reported as unknown rather than guessed. `az` is a rough angle for tuning
only (0 = front, 90 = right), because the body distorts the geometry.

**Tune it on the body.** With `DOA_PRINT 1` (the default), every loud window prints a `dir` line
with both cues, even without a detection. Wear the harness and have someone clap or play a sound
from about 2 m away: in front, to the right, behind, then to the left.

- The right answer each time means you're done. Set `DOA_PRINT 0` for the demo.
- If front and behind (or right and left) come out swapped, a mic is in a different place than
  the `MIC_*` constants in `doa.h` say. The `MODE_LEVELS` snap test shows which mic is which.
- If one cue keeps disagreeing with the truth, lower its weight with `DOA_W_TIMING` (timing
  share, 0-1). If level differences come out smaller than 6 dB, lower `DOA_LEVEL_FULL`.
- If one mic reads louder than the others for a sound from straight above, set its `MIC_TRIM_DB`.
- If you see too many `unknown` results, lower `DOA_MIN_SCORE`. If you see wrong guesses, raise it.
- `DOA_SECTORS 8` adds `FRONT-R`, `BEHIND-L` and the other diagonals. It's only worth trying once
  4 directions are reliable.

## 5. Record your own training data

`recorder.py` saves mic 1 as channel 0, which is what `convert_recordings.py` uses by default,
so these clips match what the device hears.

1. Flash `room_capture` with `MODE_STREAM` and close the Serial Monitor.
2. Record each class, playing the sound from a phone at a few distances and angles:

   ```bash
   python host/recorder.py --port COM11 --label alarm --seconds 10
   python host/recorder.py --port COM11 --label background --seconds 60
   ```

   It prints the dropped-block count and the strongest frequency on mic 1.
3. `python scripts/convert_recordings.py`, then train and export.

Every folder in `my_recordings_raw/` becomes a class, so delete or move test takes before
converting. A new folder name adds a new class. Add it to `classes.order` and `protocol.h` to
fix its position. ESC-50's `clock_alarm` is not a smoke alarm, so record a real one into
`my_recordings_raw/alarm/`.

## 6. Run the wristband

`wristband/wristband.ino` is the receiver: ESP-NOW in, motor out, OLED out, `MSG_ACK` back.
It prints its MAC and supply voltage, buzzes once at boot, and then listens. Every 5 s it also
prints `[wristband] alive ... | rx N | ... | MAC ...` on TX (115200), so a Serial Monitor opened
after boot still sees it within 5 s. A rising `rx` count means messages from the harness are
arriving.

There is no button to press, so nothing about uploading is timed to a reset: tie GPIO0 to GND
(a direct wire beats the OLED's 10 kΩ pull-ups), feed VCC from the DevKit's 3V3 pin, Upload,
then let GPIO0 go and power-cycle. The motor will twitch during the upload, because its driver
sits on GPIO3/RX and the bootloader is pushing data through that pin. The header of
`bringup/esp01_hello/esp01_hello.ino` has the same steps.

Programmer wiring (DevKit EN tied to GND holds its ESP32 in reset, leaving its USB-serial chip on
the header): DevKit 3V3 → VCC and CH_PD, GND → GND, TX0 → TX, RX0 → RX (straight through, not
crossed). To run the band while it's still on the DevKit, **unplug the RX wire first**. The
sketch turns GPIO3 into the motor output, which would fight the USB chip's transmit line. TX
alone still carries the debug output at 115200.

| On the OLED | When |
| --- | --- |
| `yamDetector / listening` | idle: link age, message count, last class and its direction, battery |
| big `SIREN`, big `BEHIND` under it, a confidence bar, `91%  alert  0.4 s` | an alert, held for 6 s (`direction unknown` when the harness couldn't tell) |
| the same with `TEST` | the harness just booted and sent `MSG_TEST` |
| `NO SIGNAL 14.2s` | nothing received for 12 s: harness off, or a channel mismatch |
| `LOW BAT` after the voltage | supply below 2.9 V, change the AAs |

- Only alert-tier classes arrive as alerts (the harness never sends the silent ones), and the
  band buzzes for `MOTOR_MS` (400 ms) showing the class name from `CLASS_NAMES`. With the
  current `ALERT_ON` that is only `SIREN` or `KNOCK`, plus `SIREN ... TEST` from the harness's
  boot message. The band knows all thirteen names, whichever model the harness runs, so changing
  `ALERT_ON` or the model never needs a wristband reflash. Custom class ids 16-31 from the laptop
  show as `CUSTOM n`.
- The band ACKs every message, alert or heartbeat, echoing `seq` and `t_ms`; that is what the
  harness's ACK line reports. It replies to the address the message came from, so neither
  sketch contains the other board's MAC.
- One `MSG_TEST` at harness boot is the fastest full-path check: plug the harness into USB,
  the band should buzz and show `TEST` within a second. The band's own boot buzz tests only the
  motor and driver.
- The radio stays awake (`WIFI_NONE_SLEEP`) so an alert never waits for a beacon; that plus the
  OLED costs roughly 90 mA, so expect half a day of demoing on two fresh AAs. Switch to
  `WIFI_MODEM_SLEEP` if you need to stretch it and can accept slower delivery.
- Knobs at the top of the sketch: `MOTOR_MS`, `ALERT_HOLD_MS`, `LINK_TIMEOUT_MS`, `LOW_BATT_MV`,
  `OLED_ADDR`.

## How the pipeline works

```text
data/ESC-50/       ── prepare_esc50.py ─────┐
data/FSD50K/       ── prepare_datasets.py ──┤──► data/processed/<class>/*.wav + manifest.csv
data/ravdess/                             ──┤                  │
my_recordings_raw/ ── convert_recordings.py ┘                  ├──► train.py  -> artifacts/model.keras
                                                               └──► export.py -> model_int8.tflite, model.h, labels.txt
```

- **Sources.** ESC-50 supplies the original alert sounds, FSD50K ten real-world alert classes
  plus speech and music, RAVDESS acted speech, and our own recordings the sound the device really
  hears. `classes.esc50_map` / `classes.fsd50k_map` say which source category becomes which
  class. FSD50K clips are multi-label: the class listed first in `classes.order` wins, so a
  `Screaming` + `Speech` clip trains as `scream`. Anything unmapped becomes `background`,
  which is where most of the room-noise variety comes from.
- **Manifest.** `manifest.csv` (`path,class,source,source_file_id,split`) is the only thing
  training reads, besides `config.yaml`. Splits are assigned per source recording (ESC-50 fold,
  FSD50K clip, RAVDESS actor), never per window, and training refuses to start if a window or a
  recording shows up in two splits.
- **Windows.** 16 kHz mono, 1 s, peak-normalized to 0.95 (max +20 dB). Near-silent windows
  are skipped for target classes. Long clips contribute at most
  `prepare.max_windows_per_clip` windows, spread over the whole clip.
- **Balance.** Every class is capped at `prepare.max_windows_per_class` windows and background
  at `prepare.background_ratio` x the average class, up to `prepare.max_background_windows`,
  per split. Speech and music have roughly 30x the source material of a siren; without the cap
  the softmax learns to answer "music". Background gets the larger ceiling because every
  background window called a target is a false buzz on the band.
  The caps are applied when the training set is built, so no files are deleted and the test
  split is reproducible.
- **Features.** TensorFlow `audio_microfrontend`: 40 channels, 30 ms window, 20 ms step,
  125-7500 Hz, log + PCAN, divided by 255. This gives `49 x 40 x 1` per window, cached under
  `data/features/` as one `.npy` each.
- **Augmentation** (train split only): background noise at -5 to 20 dB SNR, gain of -9 to +6 dB,
  and a time shift of ±250 ms. Copies are generated once and cached.
- **Model.** DS-CNN: a 3x3 conv stem (64 filters) and five depthwise-separable blocks
  (96/128/128/160/192 filters), then global average pooling and softmax. **99,661 parameters**
  (~140 KB int8, 142,952 B) and ~9.5M MACs, about 2.4x the compute of the original 5-class
  model. ReLU6 and
  BatchNorm keep the int8 conversion clean.
- **Training.** Adam with a decaying learning rate, label smoothing 0.05, square-root
  inverse-frequency class weights, and early stopping on `val_loss`. It reports per-class
  precision/recall/F1, macro F1, a confusion matrix (`artifacts/confusion_matrix.png`), the
  **false-positive rate on background**, the false alerts per hour that implies, and a
  threshold sweep showing what each threshold would do on the test split.
- **Export.** Writes float and int8 TFLite, and warns if int8 accuracy drops by more than 3
  points. Also writes `model.h` (C array plus input scale and zero point) and `labels.txt`,
  and warns when the labels or the alert tier flags differ from `protocol.h`.
- **Latest run** (13 classes, 9,234 / 2,305 / 2,204 train / val / test windows): int8 test
  accuracy **0.577** (float 0.572, so quantization costs nothing), background false-positive
  rate **0.33**, macro F1 0.45. Strongest classes are background, alarm, speech, music and
  knock; scream, car_horn and glass are the weak ones, and roughly half of FSD50K is still
  missing from `data/FSD50K/` (5,230 zero-byte files), so retraining after re-extracting it
  is the obvious next gain.

### Device / training contract

`yam_infer.h` reproduces training exactly. Change these together with the model:

1. Take 1 s of mic 1 at 16 kHz int16, after the DC blocker in `room_capture.ino`.
2. Peak-normalize it as `audio.peak_normalize` does (0.95, max +20 dB).
3. Run the microfrontend with the same parameters as `features.py`, and **reset its state for
   every window**. The Python op starts fresh, but the C frontend carries noise-reduction and
   gain state across frames.
4. Divide by `features.value_scale` (255). Quantize with
   `int8 = clip(round(f / kYamInputScale) + kYamInputZeroPoint)`, using the constants
   from `model.h`.

`libraries/microfrontend/` is the same C code the TensorFlow op runs, pinned to a TensorFlow
commit (see its `library.properties`).

## Tests

```bash
python -m unittest discover -s tests -t . -v
```

These cover window slicing, level normalization, channel selection, and the manifest logic
(fold mapping, per-file splits, per-class caps, multi-label class mapping, duplicate-path and
recording-leak checks).

## Troubleshooting

- **`audio_microfrontend op is unavailable`**: install the full `tensorflow` wheel, not a
  stripped build.
- **`could not find the ESC-50 dataset`**: the message lists every path it tried. Set
  `paths.esc50_*` in `config.yaml` or pass `--esc50-root`.
- **`warning: N FSD50K clip(s) are 0 bytes and were skipped`**: the FSD50K archive was not
  fully extracted (a half-finished copy looks like this). Re-extract it into
  `data/FSD50K/` and re-run `python scripts/prepare_datasets.py --source fsd50k`.
- **`the model header names a class that CLASS_NAMES in protocol.h doesn't list`** (harness
  boot), or **`the model has more classes than protocol.h`** (compile): the exported model has a
  class `protocol.h` doesn't know. Append it to `CLASS_NAMES`, the `CLS_` enum and `CLASS_ALERT`
  after `background`, add its `CLASS_THRESHOLD` entry, and reflash both boards.
- **Stale features after editing `config.yaml`**: this can't happen, because the cache folder
  is keyed by the feature settings. Use `--force-features` to rebuild anyway.
- **Out of memory**: lower `training.batch_size` and `augment.noise_pool_size`.
- **`recorder.py` gets no frames**: the sketch isn't in `MODE_STREAM`, or the Serial Monitor
  still holds the port.
- **Fast-blinking LED on the harness**: the PDM driver failed to start. Check pins and settings.
- **Upload says `Failed to connect`**: the band has no reset button, so there is nothing to hit
  in time. Ground GPIO0 first, then apply power while it is grounded, and only then Upload.
- **Motor buzzes while uploading**: expected. The bootloader drives GPIO3/RX, and the motor
  driver is on that pin.
- **Wristband shows `no signal yet`**: the harness isn't in `MODE_INFER` (only that mode runs
  the radio), the band is on another channel, or you are out of range. Both boards print their
  MAC and channel at boot.
- **Wristband OLED stays blank**: wrong address (`0x3C` vs `0x3D`), missing I2C pull-ups, or
  GPIO0 held low at boot. The sketch prints the failure over TX.
- **ESP-01 upload fails with "Sketch too big"**: Tools > Flash Size must be **1MB**, not 512KB.
- **Band resets when the motor spins**: supply sag. Fit the 100 µF cap, use fresh AAs, and keep
  the motor's ground return on the battery side.
